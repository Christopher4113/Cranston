## The three tour examples

### Invoice BB-2287 paid twice under two vendor names

$1,315.40 was paid to "Blue Bottle Supply" and again to "BlueBottle Supply Co" 7 days later for the same invoice BB-2287. Both payments cleared, so they match the statement, but they look like one bill paid twice under two vendor names.

- 1150 Dr 1315.40: Duplicate payment BB-2287: refund due
- 5000 Cr 1315.40: Duplicate payment BB-2287: refund due

### Rent for September was never recorded (Harbor Street Properties)

Harbor Street Properties was recorded to Rent in each of the prior months (about $4,200.00 a month), but nothing was recorded this month. Nothing hit the bank, so the reconciliation can't see it.

- 6000 Dr 4200.00: Accrue Harbor Street Properties for the month
- 2200 Cr 4200.00: Accrue Harbor Street Properties for the month

### Hiscox $6,000.00 term payment expensed at once in July

Hiscox was paid $6,000.00 for a 12-month term and expensed all at once. Spread evenly that is $500.00 a month; 9 months ($4,500.00) belong to future periods.

- 1200 Dr 4500.00: Defer unexpired Hiscox premium
- 6300 Cr 4500.00: Defer unexpired Hiscox premium

## What's hard

- **Vendor-name matching.** "Bay Area Espresso Tech" and "Bay Area Espresso Supply" score ≥0.85 on Jaro-Winkler, the same as a real near-duplicate. Name similarity alone would flag them; the check also requires the same invoice number, or the same amount with no conflicting invoice numbers.
- **Flux thresholds.** A swing is only meaningful against a clean baseline. Other findings' transactions are removed first (so the insurance prepayment doesn't also show up as a flux swing), and new memo'd items count as explained. The one false alarm shows the limit: September cups look 36% low only because August's baseline includes a legitimate $1,650 bulk order (decoy D4). A one-off in the baseline still moves the average; a median or a longer baseline would help.
- **Missing liabilities.** P14 (unremitted Q2 sales tax) is missed: nothing in the sample checklist ages balance-sheet liabilities. A "stale liability" check (balance unchanged for 60+ days) would catch it.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data, one fictional client, one quarter. Planted errors were designed by the same person who wrote the checks.
- The review rules are a small illustrative checklist with hand-picked thresholds.
- No real QuickBooks connection: imports QBO-shaped CSV exports only. Nothing is written back to a ledger.
- Explanations in this run came from: template. "template" is the deterministic no-key explainer; run with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set to regenerate with Claude (results are cached in `cache/explain/`).
